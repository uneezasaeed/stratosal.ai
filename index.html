Scientific foundation
StratoSal AI applies an interpretable machine learning model trained on seven years of ERA5 satellite climate data to predict dengue outbreak risk at district level across Punjab, Pakistan, with 86.8% validated accuracy and a 4 to 6 week lead time before surges hit. The core predictive signal: temperature above 30°C combined with anomalous monsoon rainfall reliably precedes dengue outbreak peaks, confirmed across the catastrophic 2022 (28,000 cases) and 2025 (35,000 cases) flood seasons. This lead time is sufficient for district health officers to protect children before the first patient arrives at a clinic. Malaria and chikungunya integration is in active development.
👶
Why children, and why this 4 to 6 week window matters
Children under 5 bear the highest burden of severe dengue mortality in Punjab. During the 2022 flood season, paediatric dengue cases dominated the 28,000-case surge, the worst in Pakistan's recorded history at that time. The 4 to 6 week warning window StratoSal AI provides is specifically designed to trigger pre-emptive child health actions: ORS pre-positioning, Lady Health Worker activation for household surveillance, and bed net distribution to schools and clinics. All of these interventions are deliverable within the lead time the platform generates. Every week of early warning translates directly to lives saved.
📍
Why Punjab, and why now
Punjab is Pakistan's most populous province, home to over 110 million people and the country's highest recorded dengue burden. It is also the geography where ERA5 satellite climate data, Pakistan NIH disease surveillance, and district-level health infrastructure are most accessible and reliable. Beginning with Punjab allows rigorous validation of the climate-disease forecasting model before expanding to Sindh, KPK, and broader South Asia. This is a deliberate beachhead, not a limitation.

Forecasting methodology
How the AI generates predictions
An interpretable logistic regression classifier is trained on 84 monthly observations (2019 to 2025) and predicts HIGH vs LOWER dengue outbreak risk 4 to 6 weeks ahead. The model is designed for transparency and deployability in low-resource public health settings where explainability of decisions is critical. This is a proof-of-concept pilot model; production scaling with continuously updated ERA5 data will expand the training corpus in real time. Current validated forecasting covers dengue. Malaria and chikungunya integration is in development.
